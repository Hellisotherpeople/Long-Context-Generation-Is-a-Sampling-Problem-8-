\begin{abstract}
Long-form generation in language models often collapses into repetitive loops well before the nominal context limit. This is commonly attributed to model capacity or training data, and addressed through costly retraining or post-training pipelines. We show that the sampling algorithm is a primary determinant of long-context output quality, improving outputs \textit{without} further training costs. Across ten open-weight models from 1B to 120B parameters, output lengths from 8K to 64K tokens, and more than 13{,}500 generations, full-distribution-aware samplers (P-less, Top-H, top-$n\sigma$) dominate automated metrics, while standard truncation (top-$p$, top-$k$) collapses into late-context repetition; min-$p$ falls in a middle tier. A per-step selected-token rank diagnostic isolates the mechanism: standard truncation drives the rank to the argmax floor by the final quartile, while full-distribution-aware methods stay in a healthy band across the full window. This gap that widens with model scale, establishing sampler choice as an under-appreciated bottleneck for long-context generation in open-weight models. We frame long-context generation as sampling from outside the training distribution, and encourage further study for usecases like long-context agentic coding.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
%% ====================================================================

Open-weight language models can now accept and generate very long sequences~\citep{dubey2024llama3,yang2024qwen2}.
In practice, however, long-form generation quality degrades far before the model's context limit is reached.
The back half of a 32K-token sample frequently dissolves into repeated phrases, vocabulary narrowing, and narrative drift~\citep{holtzman2020curious,welleck2020neural,fu2021theoretical}.
This degradation is commonly attributed to model capacity or training data.

We present evidence for a different explanation: \textbf{the sampling algorithm is a primary determinant of long-context output quality}.
Standard truncation methods such as top-p~\citep{holtzman2020curious} and top-k~\citep{fan2018hierarchical}, which were designed and validated on short outputs, fail systematically as generation length grows.
Adaptive methods that condition the candidate set on the shape of the token distribution at each step can prevent this collapse.

Our study spans ten instruction-tuned open-weight models across four families (Llama, Qwen, Mistral, GPT-OSS) from 1B to 120B parameters, output lengths from 8K to 64K tokens, eight fiction genres, and evaluation through automatic diversity metrics, and a 21-metric LLM-as-judge protocol with GPT-4o~\citep{zheng2023judging}.
In total, the study comprises more than 13{,}500 individual generations.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item We demonstrate that the full-distribution-aware family~\citep{tang2024topnsigma,tan2025pless,baghaei2025toph} dominates judged long-context quality at both small and frontier scale, that min-p~\citep{nguyen2024minp} sits in a middle tier, and that standard truncation collapses.
\item Building on the selected-token rank diagnostic discussed in the \pless{} literature~\citep{tan2025pless}, we trace the mechanical cause of late-context degeneration: standard truncation drives the rank to the argmax floor by the final quartile, while the full-distribution-aware family stays in the healthy 2.5--5 band throughout 64K tokens.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Background and Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
%% ====================================================================

We organize sampling methods by how much of the logit or probability distribution they use when constructing the candidate set at each decoding step.

\paragraph{Non-distribution-aware samplers.}
These methods apply a fixed rule that does not depend on the shape of the output distribution.
Temperature scaling divides logits by a constant $T$ before the softmax, uniformly stretching or compressing the distribution~\citep{ackley1985learning}.
Top-k sampling ``restricts the candidate set to the $k$ highest-probability tokens''~\citep{fan2018hierarchical}, regardless of how much probability mass they cover.
Both are the default in most inference frameworks~\citep{zheng2024sglang} and were designed and validated on short outputs.

\paragraph{Partial-distribution-aware samplers.}
These methods adapt the candidate set based on local features of the distribution, such as the maximum probability or cumulative mass, but do not use global distributional statistics.
Nucleus (top-p) sampling ``truncates to the smallest set whose cumulative probability exceeds $p$''~\citep{holtzman2020curious}, so the number of candidates varies with the distribution's concentration.
Min-p ``removes any token whose probability falls below a fraction $p_{\min}$ of the most likely token''~\citep{nguyen2024minp}, yielding a candidate set that shrinks when the model is confident and grows when it is uncertain.
Locally typical sampling selects tokens near the expected information content of the distribution~\citep{meister2023locally}.
$\eta$-sampling removes tokens below an entropy-dependent threshold~\citep{hewitt2022truncation}.
Mirostat ``directly controls the perplexity of the output stream via an adaptive feedback loop''~\citep{basu2021mirostat}.
XTC (Exclude Top Choices) promotes diversity by probabilistically excluding the highest-ranked tokens~\citep{weidmann2024xtc}.

\paragraph{Full-distribution-aware samplers.}
These methods condition the candidate set on summary statistics of the \emph{entire} logit vector, making them sensitive to the global shape of the distribution rather than only its head or cumulative mass. Top-$n\sigma$ retains tokens whose logits exceed $\mu + n\sigma$~\citep{tang2024topnsigma}. Top-H casts truncation as an entropy-constrained mass-maximization problem~\citep{baghaei2025toph}. \pless{} computes a hyperparameter-free threshold from the second moment of the distribution~\citep{tan2025pless}. Section~\ref{sec:method} formalizes all three.

\paragraph{Text degeneration and repetition.}
Neural text generation is prone to degenerate repetition during extended generation~\citep{holtzman2020curious,welleck2020neural,fu2021theoretical}. Repetition penalties~\citep{keskar2019ctrl}, diversity objectives~\citep{li2016diversity}, the DRY penalty~\citep{weidmann2024dry}, and contrastive search~\citep{su2022contrastive,su2023contrastive} target the same symptom. Our work focuses on the base truncation rule rather than these additive penalties or search strategies.

\paragraph{Why 16K--32K is long context for open-weight models.}
Frontier proprietary systems routinely produce agentic outputs of tens of thousands of tokens after extensive reinforcement learning and tool-use fine-tuning. The open-weight models in our study (1B--32B) predate that post-training pipeline. Their architectures nominally support 32K--128K context windows~\citep{dubey2024llama3,yang2024qwen2,su2024roformer}, but coherent generation at those lengths was not a training objective, so the effect of the sampling algorithm is not masked by post-training.

\paragraph{Long-context evaluation.}
Long-context capability is typically assessed through retrieval or comprehension tasks~\citep{bai2024longbench,hsieh2024ruler,shaham2023zeroscrolls}, while LongWriter~\citep{bai2025longwriter} probes length-following. LLM-as-judge evaluation~\citep{zheng2023judging,li2024generative} enables scalable quality scoring of full-length manuscripts.

\section{Full-Distribution-Aware Sampling}
\label{sec:method}
%% ====================================================================

We focus on 3 distinct full-distribution-aware samplers that condition the candidate set on summary statistics of the entire post-temperature distribution rather than only its mode or cumulative mass.

\paragraph{\pless{} sampling.}
\pless{}~\citep{tan2025pless} is a hyperparameter-free truncation rule grounded in information theory. Given the post-temperature, post-softmax probability distribution $p(v)$ over the vocabulary $V$, \pless{} computes a self-referential threshold from the second moment of the distribution and retains tokens whose probability is at least this threshold:
\begin{equation}
L[p] = \sum_{v \in V} p(v)^2,
\qquad
\mathcal{C}_{\text{p-less}} = \{v \in V : p(v) \ge L[p]\}.
\label{eq:pless}
\end{equation}
$L[p]$ is the probability that two independent samples from $p$ collide, which is also the exponential of the negative R\'enyi entropy of order 2~\citep{tan2025pless}. As the distribution becomes flatter (higher entropy), $L[p]$ shrinks and \pless{} admits more tokens. As it sharpens, $L[p]$ grows and the candidate pool tightens. \pless{} requires no hyperparameters and always presents non-empty candidate sets.

\paragraph{Top-$n\sigma$ sampling.}
Top-$n\sigma$~\citep{tang2024topnsigma} computes the standard deviation $\sigma(\mathbf{z})$ of the logit vector $\mathbf{z} \in \mathbb{R}^{|V|}$ and retains every token within $n$ standard deviations of the maximum logit:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{C}_{\text{n-sigma}} = \{v \in V : z_v \ge \max_{u} z_u - n \cdot \sigma(\mathbf{z})\}.
\label{eq:nsigma}
\end{equation}
This operates in pre-softmax logit space and exploits the empirical observation that informative logits separate from a Gaussian noise tail. Because dividing $\mathbf{z}$ by a temperature $T$ scales both $\max_u z_u$ and $\sigma(\mathbf{z})$ by the same factor, the candidate set is identical at every temperature~\citep{tang2024topnsigma}. We use $n{=}1$ throughout.

\paragraph{Top-H sampling.}
Top-H~\citep{baghaei2025toph} casts truncation as an entropy-constrained mass-maximization problem. It greedily admits tokens in descending probability order until the entropy of the truncated set reaches a target fraction $\alpha$ of the full-distribution entropy $H(p)$. We use $\alpha{=}0.4$ throughout.

\paragraph{Why these three together.}
\pless{}, top-$n\sigma$, and Top-H look at different summaries of the same global signal. \pless{} reads the second moment, top-$n\sigma$ reads the mean and standard deviation of the logits, and Top-H reads the entropy. None of them threshold against a single token's probability the way min-p does, and none cover a fixed cumulative mass the way top-p does. Our central empirical claim is that this family of full-distribution-aware methods uniformly dominates partial- and non-distribution-aware truncation in long-context generation, with all three substantially outperforming min-p and crushing top-p and top-k.

\subsection{Tasks and prompts}

We use creative fiction prompts spanning eight genres: science fiction, fantasy, mystery, horror, literary fiction, romance, thriller, and historical fiction.
We chose fiction because late-context collapse is clearly visible in long narratives: loops, scene resets, and vocabulary narrowing are easy to detect both automatically and through judging.

\subsection{Evaluation protocol}

\paragraph{Automatic metrics.}
On the full output and on each quartile we compute distinct-2~\citep{li2016diversity}, 10-gram repetition rate, and type-token ratio. Final-quartile (\Qfour{}) values capture late-context behavior after degeneration has had the most opportunity to set in.

\paragraph{Selected-token rank.}
Following the \pless{} literature~\citep{tan2025pless}, we record the rank of the actually sampled token in the descending-probability ordering of the vocabulary at each step and average over all steps. A value of $1.0$ corresponds to greedy argmax selection. Healthy Zipfian generation lives between roughly $2.5$ and $5.0$. Repetition loops drive the rank toward $1.0$ as the model's hidden states sharpen into near-deterministic confidence on the looping tokens. Comparing $Q_1$ to $Q_4$ values gives a direct readout of late-context distributional collapse.

\paragraph{LLM-as-judge.}
A 21-metric judge protocol with GPT-4o scores full-text manuscripts on coherence, long-range coherence, plot structure, pacing, prose quality, grammar and fluency, dialogue quality, creativity, world-building, characterization, non-repetitiveness, vocabulary diversity, scene diversity, engagement, emotional depth, immersion, beginning quality, middle quality, end quality, quality degradation, and overall quality. Scoring the entire output matters because many failure modes only emerge in the middle or end of a long sample~\citep{zheng2023judging}.

\section{Analysis: why full-distribution-awareness wins}
\label{sec:analysis}
%% ====================================================================

The empirical dominance of \pless{}, Top-H, and top-$n\sigma$ over Min-p, top-p, and top-k calls for a mechanical explanation. We identify two primary theoretical advantages that the full-distribution-aware family shares, illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:analysis_mechanism}.

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/fig_analysis_mechanism.png}
\caption{\textbf{The full-distribution-aware family tightens its threshold as the distribution sharpens, while top-p and top-k stay flat regardless of model state.} (a) Mean per-step truncation threshold across 32K tokens, normalized to each method's $Q_1$ value. \pless{}, Top-H, and top-$n\sigma$ all rise as the distribution sharpens through the late context. Min-p tightens partially. Top-p and top-k are flat by construction. (b) Sampler threshold versus per-step entropy $H(p)$. The full-distribution-aware family shows steep slopes against $H(p)$, min-p has a milder slope, and top-p is flat. The slope against entropy is the mechanical signature that prevents the runaway repetition loop.}
\label{fig:analysis_mechanism}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Why adaptive sampling works at all.}
Standard truncation methods fix the candidate set size regardless of model confidence.
Top-k always keeps exactly $k$ tokens, and top-p always covers the same cumulative probability mass.
In long-context generation, the model's confidence varies dynamically: it may be highly certain about function words and connectives but uncertain about content words and plot directions~\citep{meister2023locally,hewitt2022truncation}.
Adaptive methods allow the candidate set to track this variation.
When the distribution is peaked, they keep few candidates and avoid noise.
When the distribution is flat, they keep many and preserve diversity.
This is exactly the property needed to prevent the positive feedback loop where repetition narrows the distribution, which further increases repetition~\citep{fu2021theoretical}.

\paragraph{Global summary versus local peak.}
Min-p sets its truncation threshold strictly from the maximum probability $p_{\max}$, so the candidate set ignores the shape of the tail. Repetition loops begin when the model encounters subtle uncertainty over a flat distribution and standard truncation samples a token that drifts the narrative off course. The full-distribution-aware family avoids this trap by reading a global summary at every step. \pless{} reads the second moment $L[p] = \sum_v p(v)^2$~\citep{tan2025pless}, top-$n\sigma$ reads the logit mean and standard deviation~\citep{tang2024topnsigma}, and Top-H reads the entropy of the candidate set~\citep{baghaei2025toph}. All three respond to the overall sharpness or flatness of the distribution rather than its peak alone, and all three actively sense the uncertainty that triggers a loop. Min-p cannot replicate this behavior because two distributions can share the same $p_{\max}$ while having radically different global summaries.

\paragraph{The collapse loop.}
The primary failure point sits inside the sampler. When a model hallucinates or loops, it exhibits pathological confidence with selected-token rank approaching 1.0. The full-distribution-aware family acts as an automatic clutch. When the model enters an unnaturally sharp distribution, the global summary that each rule depends on (the second moment for \pless{}, $\sigma$ for top-$n\sigma$, the entropy for Top-H) shifts in the direction that makes the threshold more aggressive, prunes the noise tail, and prevents the model from sampling the very tokens that would feed the loop. By 64K tokens, this family is the only set of methods that maintains the same depth of vocabulary exploration as it had in the first paragraph.

\paragraph{The scaling trend.}
The advantage of adaptive sampling grows with model size. Standard truncation collapses further on each step up the scale, while \pless{}, Top-H, and top-$n\sigma$ remain stable. This is consistent with larger models producing sharper distributions~\citep{kaplan2020scaling}, which amplifies the failure mode of fixed-size truncation.

\paragraph{Judge-automatic metric divergence.}
Automatic diversity wins do not always translate to higher judged prose quality, especially at very high temperature where lexically diverse but incoherent text is possible~\citep{holtzman2020curious,ippolito2019comparison}. The 21-metric judge study uses a richer rubric to avoid this pitfall, and it favors the full-distribution-aware family.

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:future}
%% ====================================================================

\paragraph{Long-context generation as an out-of-distribution problem.}
Our results suggest a unifying frame: late-context generation is in part an out-of-distribution generalization problem. Open-weight base models are trained on a sequence-length distribution dominated by short documents~\citep{xiong2024effective,fu2024data}, and long-output capability in particular is bounded by the scarcity of long-output examples in SFT data~\citep{bai2025longwriter}. The back half of a 32K-token output, branching, high-entropy, full of subtle long-range dependencies, therefore sits in a region of distribution space the model has rarely seen, and length generalization in transformers is known to be brittle precisely under such distribution shifts~\citep{zhou2024transformers,huang2024formal}. From this view, full-distribution-aware samplers do not merely reduce repetition: they sample OOD more robustly by adapting the candidate set to the actual sharpness of each step's distribution rather than to a fixed assumption inherited from the in-distribution short-context regime, in line with prior framings of truncation as recovering the support of the true distribution~\citep{hewitt2022truncation}. In other words, if long-context generation is an out-of-distribution problem where long outputs are poorly represented in training data distribution, straightforward fixes such as inference-time sampling or prioritising longer training data could reliably unlock long-context performance without deeper architectural changes.

\paragraph{Do samplers improve SOTA coding agents?}
Our study targets open-weight models from 1B to 120B parameters, which predate large-scale long-context post-training which isolate the sampler effect cleanly. The natural extension is to test whether the same ranking holds on (a) newer frontier open-weight models with explicit long-context training (Kimi K2.6, GLM-5, MiniMax-class systems) and (b) agentic coding workloads, where 32K+ generations are dominated by interleaved tool calls and retrieved context rather than fiction prose. We did not run this comparison ourselves: at frontier scale, a full sampler $\times$ temperature sweep across many models with custom decoding stacks is gated by serving cost, iteration speed, and the engineering effort required to wire \pless{}, Top-H, and top-$n\sigma$ into production inference servers, since most provider APIs do not expose full-distribution-aware samplers. We view this as the most consequential next experiment in production usecases.

%% ====================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
%% ====================================================================

Sampling method choice is a first-order determinant of long-context generation quality. Standard truncation methods (top-p, top-k) suffer catastrophic final-quartile collapse at 16K, 32K, and 64K tokens, while adaptive methods prevent it. The full-distribution-aware family (\pless{}, Top-H, top-$n\sigma$) leads judged quality at small scale, dominates automated metrics at frontier scale, and remains the only family to keep the selected-token rank in its healthy band across the full 64K window. Min-p outperforms standard truncation by a wide margin and sits in a clear middle tier. Improving long-context generation does not require larger models or longer training. A better sampling algorithm can be sufficient.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Model scale range.}
The study covers ten open-weight models from 1B to 120B parameters. Closed frontier models (e.g., the GPT-4 and Claude families) cannot be tested under our protocol because their sampler implementations are not user-controllable and their decoders are not exposed to the logit processor. Our results predict but do not measure their behavior at the scales we cannot reach.

\paragraph{Domain restriction.}
All experiments use creative fiction prompts. We chose fiction because late-context collapse is clearly visible in long narratives, but other domains (technical writing, multi-turn dialogue, code generation, agentic chains) may exhibit different failure modes or interact differently with the sampler. We expect the qualitative ordering to transfer because the underlying mechanism (the global-summary feedback loop) is domain-independent, but we have not directly verified this.

\paragraph{LLM-as-judge biases.}
The 21-metric judge protocol with GPT-4o~\citep{zheng2023judging} introduces known biases (length, style preference, position effects). We do not run a human evaluation, so every quality judgment in the paper rests on automatic metrics and LLM judges.

\paragraph{Inference setup.}
Models up to 32B run with 4-bit quantization on a single RTX 5090. The 70B and 120B runs use FP8 quantization on a 4xH100 cluster. Quantization can interact with sampling at very high temperature, where logit precision matters most. We have not tested fp16 baselines for the larger models.

\paragraph{Diagnostic scope.}
The selected-token rank is a useful one-dimensional diagnostic but cannot capture every form of distributional pathology. Long-form coherence failures that do not reduce to per-step distribution sharpening (e.g., factual drift, mid-story tone shifts) are not directly measured by the rank trajectory.

\paragraph{Implementation efficiency.}
Our \pless{} implementation is an unfused custom logit processor in SGLang. Throughput overhead is modest in our setup but could matter in latency-sensitive deployments. A fused implementation would close the gap, but we do not include one.
